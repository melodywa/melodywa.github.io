citation: 'Wang, Yuxuan Melody. (resubmitted). A principle of balancing non-alternation and  alternation in tonal metres. <i>Studies in Prosodic Grammar</i>.'
abstract: 'Poetic metres govern the basic rhythmic patterns of verses and lines, and generally require the phonological property they regulate (e.g. stress, weight and/or tone) to alternate within a line to avoid monotony. Using the tonal metre of Middle Chinese regulated verse as an example, this paper proposes that a metre can also require the regulated property not to alternate, and that this requirement is balanced against alternation. The canonical templates of regulated verse require tone categories to alternate within a line, but only to a restricted extent: no non-final syllable in the templates is flanked by two syllables of the opposite category. Beyond the templates, the paper shows in Optimality Theory that the remedies prescribed by traditional prosody for irregular lines follow from the interaction of constraints encoding alternation and non-alternation. The two remedies for guping ‘Lone Even’ (孤平) are outputs of different rankings within a single, partially ranked grammar: the remedy within the line satisfies a non-alternation constraint against a lone Even tone, whereas the remedy in the other line of the couplet satisfies an alternation constraint requiring the two lines of a couplet to be tonal opposites (对). The ‘three-inversion-four-remedy’ pattern (三拗四救) arises when alternation in the cadence of a line takes priority over faithfulness. Finally, I show that this balancing property enriches the typology of poetic metres and parallels restrictions found in the tonal phonology of natural languages, and that metres can be informed by – and thus serve as a window into – natural language phonology.'
