abstract: 'Self-destructive feeding (SDF) is a type of overapplication opacity in which, in rule-based terms, an earlier rule creates the environment for a later rule that in turn removes part of the environment that motivated the earlier one. This paper re-examines four cases of SDF (two in Turkish, one in Javanese and one in Japanese) and shows that they can be accounted for in Parallel Optimality Theory. After introducing the four cases, the paper presents the two properties they share. First, at least one of the processes involved in each interaction is restricted to morpheme junctures and particular morphemes. Second, they resolve consonant sequences at stem-suffix junctures by realising a suffix-initial segment in required contexts. I propose that the alternating segments are underspecified for their association to the skeletal tier, whereas non-alternating segments are fully-specified. In Parallel Optimality Theory, faithfulness to association in general and stronger faithfulness in certain input contexts or positions then account for the lack of morpheme-internal alternations and the SDF outputs in a single input-output evaluation. The three SDF cases involving consonants follow from the rankings needed for the simple alternations alone, and the vowel case requires only one additional ranking. Therefore,SDF involving alternations with zero can be derived in Parallel Optimality Theory once the underlying representations of segments are more appropriately understood.'
